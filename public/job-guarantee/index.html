THE THESIS

We do not lack useful work. We lack a durable way to fund, organize and fairly compensate it.

The operating model

Not a temporary jobs bill.
A permanent institution.

The program expands when private employment contracts and shrinks as private employers hire workers away. Funding follows enrollment instead of limiting the number of available jobs.

01

Make the offer universal

No employability ranking and no lottery. Eligible adults who want work receive an offer; participation remains voluntary.

02

Keep a local project bank

Schools, tribes, municipalities and qualified nonprofits maintain approved, shelf-ready projects before unemployment rises.

03

Set a labor standard

A common wage and benefit package becomes the effective floor private employers must beat to recruit workers.

04

Let enrollment float

Spending rises automatically in a downturn and recedes during recovery. The job remains available without a new act of Congress.

V1965

An American precedent

VISTA proved the architecture.
It also exposed the gap.

Born in the War on Poverty, Volunteers in Service to America placed federally supported people inside local organizations to build community capacity. Its early purpose emphasized organizing, empowerment and attacking the causes—not only the symptoms—of poverty.

WHAT IT PROVED

Federal resources can support locally directed, socially useful work through community institutions.

WHAT IT LEFT UNDONE

Selective entry, temporary terms and subsistence allowances are not a right to secure employment.

A Job Guarantee would preserve VISTA’s community-rooted capacity building while turning sacrifice into employment: wages, benefits, accessibility and an offer available to everyone.

The demand question

How do we know these jobs are needed?

There is no honest single estimate of “available Job Guarantee jobs.” Need, funded demand and organizational capacity are different quantities. A credible program measures all three.

VISIBLE GAPHigh

Care and human services

Aging, disability support, childcare and family caregiving produce recurring labor needs—but also require screening, training and continuity.

VISIBLE GAPHigh

Education support

Tutoring, attendance outreach, after-school programs and classroom assistance can reduce teacher overload without replacing licensed educators.

VISIBLE GAPPlace-based

Climate and remediation

Heat, floods, fires, polluted land and neglected public space create a large backlog whose labor requirements vary sharply by location.

BINDING GAPConstraint

Supervision

The hardest shortage may be people and institutions capable of designing projects, supporting workers and certifying useful output.

Measure the gap locally.

Each sponsor should publish its unmet-service baseline, proposed output, worker capacity, supervisory ratio and material requirements. “We need help” becomes an auditable project—not a rhetorical estimate.

Economic transmission

What changes beyond the jobs themselves?

AUTOMATIC STABILIZER

Employment absorbs the shock

The program expands when private hiring falls and contracts as private employers recruit from it.

LABOR STANDARD

The floor becomes a real option

The wage and benefits establish what workers can refuse, not merely what employers are prohibited from paying.

SECTORAL BALANCES

Deficits support private income

Program spending adds income to the nongovernment sector. Taxes and hiring out of the program moderate the effect as activity recovers.

Failure modes

Design against the obvious risks.

The program succeeds only if it creates additional, decent work without becoming a low-wage shadow public sector.

01

Displacement

Maintenance-of-effort rules and union review prevent sponsors from replacing existing employees or funded vacancies.

02

Make-work

Projects publish outputs, beneficiaries and evaluations. Workers can transfer out of projects that lack useful activity.

03

Inflation and bottlenecks

Favor labor-intensive projects, map scarce inputs, phase material-heavy work and let the base wage anchor the offer.

04

Administrative overload

Fund planners and supervisors first, certify projects continuously and build capacity before the next downturn.

05

Exclusion

Provide part-time schedules, childcare, transportation, disability accommodations and jobs that match different abilities.

A credible path

Build the institution before promising the right.

The employment offer can become universal only when every region can reliably convert enrollment into safe, useful work.

  1. YEAR 0Fund local planning

    Map unmet needs, certify sponsors and hire supervisors.

  2. YEAR 1Launch diverse pilots

    Urban, rural, tribal and high-unemployment regions.

  3. YEAR 2Guarantee priority groups

    Test open enrollment, worker choice and appeals.

  4. YEAR 3+Establish the national right

    Funding automatically follows qualified enrollment.
