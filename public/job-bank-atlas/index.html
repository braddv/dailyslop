SUPPLY LAYER

Where employment is needed

  • Monthly BLS unemployment rate and count
  • Long-duration and recurring unemployment
  • Participation and employment-population gaps
  • Metro, county and commuting-area boundaries

NEED LAYER

Where useful work is waiting

  • Childcare, eldercare and disability-service gaps
  • Tutoring, attendance and after-school demand
  • Heat, fire, flood and weatherization backlogs
  • Parks, food access and public-health capacity

DELIVERY LAYER

Where a job can start safely

  • Approved public or nonprofit sponsor
  • Named supervisor and worker-support ratio
  • Training, equipment and accessibility plan
  • Published output and non-displacement review

What the atlas should return

A ranked investigation list—not a fake jobs number.

The atlas should only estimate positions after local sponsors document a need and their delivery capacity. Until then, it identifies where planning grants and project development are most urgent.

Build sequence

Start with evidence we already have.

  1. NOWState unemployment layer

    Monthly BLS rates reveal broad geographic differences.

  2. NEXTCounty labor-market layer

    Add unemployment counts, persistence and participation without confusing seasonally adjusted and unadjusted data.

  3. THENCommunity-need indicators

    Combine federal datasets with locally validated service gaps.

  4. REQUIREDSponsor project registry

    No job estimate becomes actionable until a qualified institution can supervise it.

  5. FINALVoluntary worker matching

    American Job Center intake connects preferences and accommodations to real approved offers.
