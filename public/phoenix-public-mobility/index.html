The main plan

Rail spine.
Rapid grid.
Shared fleet.

This is not 359,000 cars wandering independently. The system pools the thickest commute flows into rail, autonomous buses and vans; small robotaxis fill the geographic and accessibility gaps.

Give pooled vans and regional buses priority in existing HOV lanes during peaks. The model assumes no new freeway lanes.

Cars handle low-density and accessible trips; pooled vans and autonomous buses absorb commute demand and feed rail.

A morning in the network

Mesa home.
Tempe job.

The rider requests one trip—not three separate services. The public dispatcher chooses the fastest pooled itinerary, guarantees each transfer and charges one fare.

  1. 01Front door

    A pooled autonomous van meets the rider near home in Mesa. No household car, parking hunt or fixed-route detour.

  2. 02Gilbert Road station

    The van groups nearby riders headed west and delivers them to the A Line immediately before the train arrives.

  3. 03Rail to Tempe

    Light rail carries the concentrated flow past congestion to Downtown Tempe or ASU without adding another car to US-60 or Loop 202.

  4. 04Last mile

    Walk to a nearby workplace or board a waiting shared vehicle for the final connection. The same app protects the transfer.

The operating rule: the dispatcher should not send a solo Waymo from Mesa to Tempe when a pooled feeder plus rail can provide a competitive trip. Convenience stays door-to-door; capacity comes from sharing the middle.
